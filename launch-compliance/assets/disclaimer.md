## About these documents

These documents were drafted from what your codebase actually does — the data
your schema stores, the third-party services you call, the integrations in your
dependency list — rather than from a generic template. That makes them a much
better starting point than boilerplate, and it is why they name specific
services and specific retention periods.

**They are not legal advice, and I am not your lawyer.** Specifically:

- **They describe your practices as they appeared at the time of the scan.**
  If anything changes — a new analytics tool, a new country, a new AI feature —
  they become inaccurate, and an inaccurate policy is worse than no policy. The
  review triggers in `COMPLIANCE.md` are there for this.
- **Anything flagged as needing specialist review genuinely needs it.** Health
  data, children's data, biometrics, lending, insurance, employment decisions
  and similar categories carry obligations that no template can discharge.
- **Read them before you publish them.** You are the one making the statements
  in them. If a sentence isn't true of your product, cut it — that single check
  is worth more than everything else in this document set.

If you're taking money, holding sensitive data, raising investment, or signing
a customer with their own contract, have a lawyer read what you publish. It is
a cheaper conversation than the alternative, and a specific, accurate draft
makes it a shorter one.
